Ray & sphere intersection

The rayray.h
\[ \class{mjxvar v-P}{P}(\class{mjxvar v-t}{t}) = \class{mjxvar v-O}{O} + \class{mjxvar v-t}{t}\,\class{mjxvar v-d}{d} \]

A point, plus a scalar multiple of a direction. Every ray in the renderer — camera rays and bounced rays alike — is this one equation.

Sphere surface conditionsphere.h
\[ |\class{mjxvar v-P}{P} - \class{mjxvar v-C}{C}|^2 = \class{mjxvar v-r}{r}^2 \]

Any point \(P\) on the sphere's surface is exactly \(r\) away from the center \(C\).

Solving for the hit distancesphere.h :: hit()
\[ a = \class{mjxvar v-d}{d}\cdot \class{mjxvar v-d}{d}, \qquad h = \class{mjxvar v-d}{d}\cdot(\class{mjxvar v-C}{C}-\class{mjxvar v-O}{O}), \qquad c = (\class{mjxvar v-C}{C}-\class{mjxvar v-O}{O})\cdot(\class{mjxvar v-C}{C}-\class{mjxvar v-O}{O}) - \class{mjxvar v-r}{r}^2 \] \[ \class{mjxvar v-t}{t} = \frac{h \pm \sqrt{h^2 - ac}}{a} \]

Substituting \(P(t)\) into the surface condition and expanding gives a quadratic in \(t\); this is its "half-\(b\)" simplified form. Take the smaller root that still falls within the valid \(t\) range.

Normals & material directions

Outward normalsphere.h :: hit()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-n}{n} = \frac{\class{mjxvar v-P}{P} - \class{mjxvar v-C}{C}}{\class{mjxvar v-r}{r}} \]

Dividing by the radius both points outward and normalizes it to unit length in one step.

Front-face fliphittable.h :: set_face_normal()
\[ n_{\text{used}} = \begin{cases} \class{mjxvar v-n}{n} & \class{mjxvar v-d}{d} \cdot \class{mjxvar v-n}{n} < 0 \\ -\class{mjxvar v-n}{n} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Keeps the normal always facing against the incoming ray, and records whether the ray hit the outside or inside of the surface — dielectrics need to know which.

Reflectionvec3.h :: reflect()
\[ \operatorname{reflect}(\class{mjxvar v-d}{d}, \class{mjxvar v-n}{n}) = \class{mjxvar v-d}{d} - 2(\class{mjxvar v-d}{d} \cdot \class{mjxvar v-n}{n})\,\class{mjxvar v-n}{n} \]

Mirrors \(d\) about the surface normal. Used directly by metal, and as the fallback branch of dielectric.

Snell's law & vector refractionvec3.h :: refract()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-etai}{\eta_i} \sin\theta_i = \class{mjxvar v-etat}{\eta_t} \sin\theta_t \] \[ \class{mjxvar v-rperp}{r_{\perp}} = \class{mjxvar v-eta}{\eta}\,(\class{mjxvar v-d}{d} + \cos\theta_i\, \class{mjxvar v-n}{n}), \qquad \class{mjxvar v-rpar}{r_{\parallel}} = -\sqrt{1 - |\class{mjxvar v-rperp}{r_{\perp}}|^2}\;\class{mjxvar v-n}{n} \] \[ \operatorname{refract}(\class{mjxvar v-d}{d}, \class{mjxvar v-n}{n}, \class{mjxvar v-eta}{\eta}) = \class{mjxvar v-rperp}{r_{\perp}} + \class{mjxvar v-rpar}{r_{\parallel}} \]

How light bends passing into a denser or thinner medium. The vector form splits the refracted ray into components perpendicular and parallel to the normal.

Schlick's reflectance approximationmaterial.h :: reflectance()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-R}{R_0} = \left(\frac{1-\class{mjxvar v-eta}{\eta}}{1+\class{mjxvar v-eta}{\eta}}\right)^2, \qquad \class{mjxvar v-R}{R}(\class{mjxvar v-theta}{\theta}) = \class{mjxvar v-R}{R_0} + (1-\class{mjxvar v-R}{R_0})(1-\cos\class{mjxvar v-theta}{\theta})^5 \]

The probability glass reflects rather than refracts, as a function of viewing angle — why glass looks mirror-like at a grazing angle and transparent head-on.

Lambertian (diffuse) scattermaterial.h :: lambertian::scatter()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-d}{d'} = \class{mjxvar v-n}{n} + \class{mjxvar v-xi}{\xi}, \qquad \class{mjxvar v-xi}{\xi} \sim \text{Uniform(unit sphere)} \]

Normal plus a random unit vector — biases the bounce toward "outward-ish," approximating a cosine-weighted distribution without any trigonometry.

Metal (fuzzy specular) scattermaterial.h :: metal::scatter()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-d}{d'} = \operatorname{reflect}(\class{mjxvar v-dhat}{\hat d}, \class{mjxvar v-n}{n}) + \class{mjxvar v-fuzz}{\text{fuzz}} \cdot \class{mjxvar v-xi}{\xi} \]

A perfect mirror reflection, jittered by a random vector scaled by the roughness parameter \(\text{fuzz} \in [0,1]\).

Camera & viewport geometry

Viewport size from field of viewcamera.h :: initialize()
\[ h = \tan\!\left(\frac{\class{mjxvar v-vfov}{\theta_{vfov}}}{2}\right) \] \[ \text{viewport}_{\text{height}} = 2h \cdot \text{focus}_{\text{dist}} \] \[ \text{viewport}_{\text{width}} = \text{viewport}_{\text{height}} \cdot \frac{\text{image}_{\text{width}}}{\text{image}_{\text{height}}} \]

A wider vertical FOV or a farther focus distance both make the virtual rectangle the camera looks through larger.

Camera basis vectorscamera.h :: initialize()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-w}{w} = \widehat{\text{lookfrom} - \text{lookat}}, \qquad \class{mjxvar v-u}{u} = \widehat{\text{vup} \times \class{mjxvar v-w}{w}}, \qquad \class{mjxvar v-v}{v} = \class{mjxvar v-w}{w} \times \class{mjxvar v-u}{u} \]

Builds an orthonormal right/up/backward frame from just a position, a target, and a rough "up" direction — this is what lets the camera sit and point anywhere.

Pixel position on the viewportcamera.h :: get_ray()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-P}{P_{i,j}} = \class{mjxvar v-P}{P_{00}} + \class{mjxvar v-i}{i}\,\class{mjxvar v-du}{\Delta u} + \class{mjxvar v-j}{j}\,\class{mjxvar v-dv}{\Delta v} \]

Walk \(i\) steps right and \(j\) steps down from pixel \((0,0)\)'s world-space position to find any pixel's position.

Defocus (depth-of-field) ray origincamera.h :: defocus_disk_sample()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-O}{O'} = \class{mjxvar v-Ccam}{C_{\text{cam}}} + \class{mjxvar v-p}{p_x} \cdot \class{mjxvar v-disku}{\text{disk}_u} + \class{mjxvar v-p}{p_y} \cdot \class{mjxvar v-diskv}{\text{disk}_v}, \qquad \class{mjxvar v-p}{p} \sim \text{Uniform(unit disk)} \]

Instead of every ray starting from one exact point, it starts from a random point on a small disk — blurring anything not exactly at the focus distance, like a real lens.

Monte Carlo sampling & recursive shading

Per-pixel Monte Carlo averagecamera.h :: render()
\[ \text{Color}_{\text{pixel}} = \frac{1}{\class{mjxvar v-N}{N}}\sum_{s=1}^{\class{mjxvar v-N}{N}} \class{mjxvar v-L}{L}(\text{ray}_{\class{mjxvar v-s}{s}}) \]

Trace \(N\) independently jittered rays through the same pixel and average the results — this single average is what removes noise, antialiases edges, and softens shadows and reflections, all at once.

Recursive path radiancecamera.h :: ray_color()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-L}{L}(\text{ray}, \class{mjxvar v-depth}{d}) = \begin{cases} 0 & \class{mjxvar v-depth}{d} = 0 \quad \text{(out of bounces)} \\[4pt] \class{mjxvar v-A}{A} \cdot \class{mjxvar v-L}{L}(\text{ray}_{\text{scattered}}, \class{mjxvar v-depth}{d}-1) & \text{hit, scattered} \\[4pt] 0 & \text{hit, absorbed} \\[4pt] L_{\text{sky}}(\class{mjxvar v-dhat}{\hat d}) & \text{miss} \end{cases} \]

The whole path tracer in one recursive definition: each bounce multiplies in that surface's attenuation \(A\), until the path escapes to the sky, gets absorbed, or runs out of bounce budget.

Sky background gradientcamera.h :: ray_color()
\[ a = \tfrac12(\class{mjxvar v-dhat}{\hat d}_y + 1), \qquad \text{Color}_{\text{sky}} = (1-a)\cdot\text{white} + a\cdot\text{skyblue} \]

The renderer's only light source: a simple top-to-bottom linear blend, keyed off how "upward" the missed ray points.

Acceleration structure

AABB slab test, per axis \(k\)aabb.h :: hit()
\[ \class{mjxvar v-t}{t_0} = \frac{\text{box}_{\min,\class{mjxvar v-k}{k}} - \class{mjxvar v-O}{O}_{\class{mjxvar v-k}{k}}}{\class{mjxvar v-d}{d}_{\class{mjxvar v-k}{k}}}, \qquad \class{mjxvar v-t}{t_1} = \frac{\text{box}_{\max,\class{mjxvar v-k}{k}} - \class{mjxvar v-O}{O}_{\class{mjxvar v-k}{k}}}{\class{mjxvar v-d}{d}_{\class{mjxvar v-k}{k}}} \] \[ [\class{mjxvar v-t}{t_{\min}}, \class{mjxvar v-t}{t_{\max}}] \leftarrow [\class{mjxvar v-t}{t_{\min}}, \class{mjxvar v-t}{t_{\max}}] \cap \bigl[\min(\class{mjxvar v-t}{t_0},\class{mjxvar v-t}{t_1}),\, \max(\class{mjxvar v-t}{t_0},\class{mjxvar v-t}{t_1})\bigr] \]

Repeat for \(k = x, y, z\), shrinking the valid interval each time. If it ever becomes empty, the ray missed the box — and everything inside it is skipped for free.

Color output

Gamma correctioncolor.h :: linear_to_gamma()
\[ c_{\text{gamma}} = \sqrt{c_{\text{linear}}} \]

A \(\gamma \approx 2\) approximation of sRGB. Skipping this step is why an un-gamma-corrected render looks unexpectedly dark in the midtones.
